## Company Overview

### Brand Meaning

Since May 2025, the name INFINEARTH combines “Infinity” and “Earth.” The stated mission is to create a sustainable society in harmony with the environment through technology. The stated slogan is “TECHNOLOGY FOR THE EARTH.”

The company was known as Daihatsu Diesel Mfg. Co., Ltd. from its establishment in 1966 until its name changed to Daihatsu Infinearth Mfg. Co., Ltd. in May 2025. Its legacy origin is connected to Hatsudoki Manufacturing Co., Ltd., founded in 1907.

## Company History

### Legacy Origin and Establishment: 1907–1969

- 1907: Hatsudoki Manufacturing Co., Ltd., the legacy company associated with the later Daihatsu organization, was founded.
- May 1966: The Osaka division of Daihatsu Motor Co., Ltd., which manufactured marine and general-purpose diesel engines, was separated and Daihatsu Diesel Mfg. Co., Ltd. was established.
- August 1968: Nagasaki Marine Service Co., Ltd. was established as a distributor of internal-combustion engines in Kyushu. It later became Daihatsu Infinearth Nishinihon Co., Ltd., a consolidated subsidiary.
- August 1969: The Moriyama Factory in Shiga Prefecture began operations.

### Expansion and Distribution: 1970–1989

- April 1970: The company merged to change the par value of its shares; the registered establishment date became April 29, 1939. Daihatsu Diesel Imabari Service Co., Ltd. was also established for Shikoku and later became Daihatsu Infinearth Shikoku Co., Ltd.
- September 1971: The Moriyama Factory received permission from Japan's Minister of Transport to display Japanese Industrial Standards for marine four-stroke diesel main engines.
- December 1972: Daihatsu Diesel Nakanihon Co., Ltd. was established for the Kinki, Chugoku, and San'in regions and later became a Daihatsu Infinearth consolidated subsidiary.
- November 1977: The company was listed on the Second Section of the Osaka Securities Exchange.
- January 1979: The Moriyama Second Factory began operations. East Japan Daihatsu Diesel Sales Co., Ltd. was established for Kanto, Tohoku, and Hokkaido and later became Daihatsu Infinearth East Japan Co., Ltd.
- September 1981: Daihatsu Diesel (U.S.A.), Inc. was established as the distributor for North and Central America and later became Daihatsu Infinearth (America), Inc.
- December 1981: Daihatsu Diesel Transport Co., Ltd., later DS Corporation, was established for coating, packaging, and transporting internal-combustion engines.
- January 1982: A technical license agreement was concluded with China Shipbuilding & Engineering Co.
- August 1982: Daihatsu Diesel (Singapore) Pte. Ltd. was established as the general sales distributor for Asia and later became Daihatsu Infinearth (Asia Pacific) Pte. Ltd.
- August 1983: A parts center and research factory were constructed at the Moriyama Second Factory.
- October 1983: Daihatsu Diesel (Europe) Ltd. was established as the European distributor and later became Daihatsu Infinearth (Europe) Ltd.
- March 1984: Production and sales of TX-series gas-turbine power-generation equipment began.
- September 1986: DD Parts Service Co., Ltd., later Daihatsu Infinearth Parts Service Co., Ltd., was established as a parts sales company in the Kinki region.
- November 1986: A precision-machinery and mechatronics factory was constructed at the Moriyama Second Factory, and production began.
- October 1988: An aluminum-products plant was completed at the Moriyama Second Factory.

### Headquarters and Product Growth: 1990–2000

- September 1990: The head office was relocated from Kita-ku to Chuo-ku in Osaka during the Shin-Umeda City development.
- November 1991: Daihatsu Diesel Umeda City Co., Ltd., later Daihatsu Infinearth Umeda City Co., Ltd., was established for redevelopment of the former head-office site and operation of rental offices and parking.
- January 1993: Manufacturing and sales of the high-power DK-20 and DK-28 diesel-engine series began.
- March 1993: Construction of the jointly developed Umeda Sky Building on the former head-office site was completed.
- December 1993: The Shanghai Office opened in China.
- January 1994: The company obtained ISO 9001 certification.
- March 1997: Production and sales of the 12DK-36, described as the company's largest-output engine at the time, began.
- August 1998: A vertical-shaft L-type gas turbine for pump drives was developed.
- February 1999: A sales contract for small gas turbines was signed with Toyota Turbine & Systems Inc.
- May 2000: Daihatsu Diesel (Shanghai) Co., Ltd. was established as the general distributor in China and later became Daihatsu Infinearth (Shanghai) Co., Ltd.
- December 2000: Sales of food-waste treatment equipment began, and the Moriyama Factory obtained ISO 14001 certification.

### Technical Development and International Cooperation: 2001–2009

- October 2001: A technical development center and archives were constructed at the Moriyama First Factory.
- December 2001: A diesel-engine technical cooperation agreement was concluded with HSD Engine Co., Ltd. of Korea.
- January 2003: NKK Corporation, later JFE Engineering Corporation, and Iwatani Corporation began joint development of a large diesel power-generation system using dimethyl ether fuel.
- April 2004: The Taiwan Office opened.
- June 2004: Manufacturing and sales of the next-generation, fuel-efficient DC-32 diesel engine began.
- October 2004: A gas-engine technical cooperation agreement was concluded with Mitsui Engineering & Shipbuilding Co., Ltd., later MITSUI E&S Co., Ltd.
- July 2005: The head office moved from Chuo-ku to Kita-ku in Osaka, returning to the area where the company was founded.
- September 2005: The Moriyama Factory obtained China Classification Society factory certification.
- August 2006: Cumulative diesel-engine production reached 30 million horsepower.
- July 2007: Daihatsu Diesel Anqing Ironworks Co., Ltd. was established as a joint venture with Anqing CSSC Diesel Engine Co., Ltd.
- September 2007: A technical cooperation agreement for super-clean marine diesel engines was concluded with the Japan Ship Machinery and Equipment Association.
- May 2008: The R&D Center was constructed at the Moriyama Second Factory.

### Emissions and New Engine Development: 2010–2014

- June 2010: The first 6DK-20e engine compliant with Tier II NOx regulations was shipped.
- July 2010: A license agreement was concluded with Doosan Engine Co., Ltd. of Korea.
- August 2010: A technical cooperation agreement was concluded with Alfa Diesel of Brazil.
- September 2010: MD Engineering Co., Ltd. was established as a joint venture with Mitsui Engineering & Shipbuilding Co., Ltd., later MITSUI E&S Co., Ltd.
- November 2010: A launch event was held for the environmentally focused DE-18 and DE-23 engines.
- July 2011: A license agreement was concluded with Kirloskar Oil Engines Ltd. of India.
- October 2011: The first DE-18 engine was shipped.
- November 2011: The first DE-23 engine was shipped.
- March 2012: A license agreement was concluded with a Chinese technical partner for the MD-G gas-engine series jointly developed with Mitsui Engineering & Shipbuilding Co., Ltd.
- July 2012: A license agreement was concluded with Alfa Diesel of Brazil.
- March 2013: Design of a marine selective catalytic reduction system lineup was completed.
- July 2013: Following market consolidation, the company's listing moved from the Osaka Securities Exchange Second Section to the Tokyo Stock Exchange Second Section.

### Monitoring, Dual Fuel, and Modernization: 2015–2025

- June 2015: Shares in Nippon Nozzle Seiki Co., Ltd. were acquired.
- November 2015: Daihatsu Diesel Himeji Mfg. Co., Ltd., later Daihatsu Infinearth Himeji Mfg. Co., Ltd., was established.
- July 2017: The first order was received for a maintenance-support service using the C-MAXS LC-A next-generation condition-monitoring system for marine auxiliary engines.
- October 2017: The first marine SCR system compliant with IMO NOx Tier III regulations was shipped.
- November 2017: The general building was constructed at the Moriyama First Factory.
- February 2018: The first 6DE28DF dual-fuel engine was shipped.
- August 2018: The Himeji Factory was completed and began operations.
- 2021: The earlier supplied summary states that the LNG dual-fuel engine lineup was completed.
- April 2022: Following a market-classification review, the company transferred from the Tokyo Stock Exchange Second Section to the Standard Market.
- May 2025: The company name changed to Daihatsu Infinearth Mfg. Co., Ltd.
